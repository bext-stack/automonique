## Objective

Define the authorization vocabulary the whole product is judged against:
tenant-scoped actors, explicitly mapped external identities, historical role
grants and policy revisions, credential descriptors that never carry a secret
value, the authorization evidence recorded with every decision, and the
break-glass record that makes emergency access auditable rather than invisible.

This slice defines identity values, policy evaluation and evidence only. It
authenticates nobody, resolves no credential, contacts no identity provider,
reads no database and grants no session. It is the layer that makes an
unauthorized state unrepresentable, so that later layers cannot invent
authority they were never given.

## Identity contract

- Every actor is tenant-scoped. A tenant-free actor is unrepresentable, and an
  actor identifier from one tenant cannot be compared equal to, or substituted
  for, one from another. Cross-tenant comparison is a typed error rather than
  a `false` result, because silently returning "not equal" is how a
  cross-tenant check becomes a no-op.
- External identities — Slack, Telegram, GitHub, Teams, Discord, Support and
  SDK — are mapped explicitly to an actor. The mapping key includes platform,
  application, external tenant or installation and the platform's immutable
  user identifier. Mutable attributes such as UPN, email, display name,
  username and roles are attributes, never keys and never authority.
- Role grants and policy revisions are historical records, not mutable current
  state: a grant carries its revision, the granting actor, the effective window
  and the reason. Asking "was this permitted at revision N" is answerable from
  the record, so an authorization decision made in the past stays explainable
  after the policy changes.
- Policy evaluation is deny-by-default and total. An unmatched request is
  denied with a typed reason; there is no fall-through to allow, no path where
  an evaluation error yields permission, and no wildcard grant. Evaluation is
  pure: the same request against the same policy revision yields the same
  decision forever.
- Every decision produces authorization evidence naming the actor, tenant,
  policy revision, matched grant and outcome. A decision without evidence is
  unrepresentable, so an allow can always be explained and an audit cannot find
  a permission with no origin.
- Credentials appear only as descriptors: owner, purpose, audience, creation,
  expiry or rotation deadline, version and health state. A secret value is not
  representable in any identity or policy type — proven by compile-fail
  coverage — and descriptors are never usable as credentials themselves.
  Rotation is expressible with an overlap window; revocation immediately
  invalidates derived capabilities.
- Break-glass access is a first-class recorded action carrying the invoking
  actor, the reason, an expiry and the elevated grant. It cannot be silent, it
  cannot be unbounded in time, and restarting the system does not clear it —
  the record and its expiry are durable values, not process state.

## Verification contract

| Check | Expected |
|---|---|
| Tenant scoping | a tenant-free actor is unrepresentable; cross-tenant comparison is a typed error rather than a `false` result |
| External identity keys | mapping keys use platform, application, external tenant/installation and immutable user ID; UPN, email, display name, username and roles are rejected as keys |
| Historical grants | "was this permitted at revision N" is answerable from the record after later policy change, proven over a synthetic revision history |
| Deny by default | unmatched requests deny with a typed reason; no fall-through, no wildcard grant, and no path where an evaluation error yields permission |
| Evaluation purity | identical request and policy revision yield identical decisions across runs and orderings |
| Evidence completeness | every decision carries actor, tenant, policy revision, matched grant and outcome; a decision without evidence is unrepresentable |
| Secret unrepresentability | no identity or policy type can hold a secret value, proven by compile-fail coverage; a descriptor cannot be used as a credential |
| Rotation and revocation | an overlap window is expressible; revocation invalidates derived capabilities immediately |
| Break-glass audit | break-glass carries actor, reason, expiry and grant; it is durable, bounded in time and cannot be constructed silently |
| Quality | workspace tests, strict Clippy and formatting pass offline with no new allowance, network access, runtime dependency or lockfile drift |

## Compatibility and evidence

Use only the permitted product requirements, the structural migration map and
synthetic fixtures.
`docs/product-plan/requirements/state-and-protocols.md` § Identity, approvals
and controller leases and § External connector installations, plus
`docs/product-plan/requirements/operations-and-governance.md` § Configuration
and credential lifecycle, are the sources for tenant scoping, the external
identity key rule and the descriptor model. Record the decision case matrix,
the deny-reason category table and the compile-fail cases proving secret
unrepresentability. Use synthetic identities only; no real workspace, tenant or
account name appears in a fixture.

## Stop conditions

Stop on any need for a new dependency or `Cargo.lock` change, a representable
tenant-free actor, cross-tenant comparison returning `false` instead of
erroring, a mutable attribute used as an identity key, a wildcard or
fall-through allow, any path where an evaluation error grants permission, a
decision without evidence, a secret value becoming representable, an unbounded
or silent break-glass grant, an identity-provider or database call, a
licence-boundary violation, a forbidden clean-room input, or any path outside
the lease.
